Harvesting vegetables successfully needs neither a big garden nor much work watering. In this
blog post I describe how to build a planter with long-term irrigation out of a **30 l plastic
container** (a flower pot in this example), an **onion planting tray**, some **fabric** (from
an old pair of jeans, for instance) and a **garden fleece**. A drill or a cordless
screwdriver is a bonus, as it makes the drainage hole easier to make.

When choosing the plastic container, make sure there are no holes in the bottom (or seal them
with epoxy resin). The onion planting tray goes into the pot upside down and should have
roughly the same diameter as the pot.

![Plastic flower pot with onion planting tray](/img/blog/plastikblumentopf-mit-zwiebelpflanzschale_14.jpeg "Plastic flower pot with onion planting tray")

The onion planting tray separates the water reservoir from the growing medium.

Now drill a hole (6 mm) in the side at the height of the onion planting tray. This acts as an
overflow so the plant does not stand in water. The planter can later be filled with water up
to this point. To draw the water up out of the reservoir I used strips of fabric from an old
pair of trousers as a wick and hung them into the planting tray.

![Fabric strips as a wick](/img/blog/stoffstreifen-als-docht_7.jpeg "Fabric strips as a wick")

The two fabric strips carry the water up into the growing medium.

All that remains is a garden fleece, so the growing medium does not later fall into the water
reservoir. It is worth pushing a plastic pipe cut off at an angle into the SIP for filling it
with water later. It makes watering easier.

![Finished SIP with root fleece](/img/blog/fertiger-sip-mit-wurzelvlies_4.jpeg "Finished SIP with root fleece")

The garden fleece is the separating layer between the water supply and the growing medium.

Now the **sub-irrigated planter** can be planted up. Ordinary vegetable soil, fertilised
occasionally, works fine as the medium. At the start, water the SIP normally (making sure the
reservoir is always full) until the roots have developed and can draw water from below better.
After that it is enough to fill the reservoir through a plastic pipe and a funnel. If it is
not too hot, you only need to water every few days.
